# What the diffs show so far

First pass over `ncert-diff diff` output, October 2026. Page numbers are printed pages of the
earlier edition in each pair. Everything below was read from the generated hunks and then
checked against the chapter text, except where marked as needing a look at the page image.
This is a record of what changed, not a judgement of why.

## Our Pasts II (Class 7)

### 2007 text (2014 capture) to 2013 reprint (2016 capture)

Mostly copy-editing, with a few substantive edits:

- Chapter 4 retitled from "The Creation of an Empire: The Mughal Dynasty" to "The Mughal Empire".
- Chapter 1, p. 12: Allah's "love, mercy and beauty" became "love, mercy and bounty". "The
  Prophet's son-in-law" became "the Prophet Muhammad's son-in-law".
- Chapter 1, p. 6: the second version of Abdul Hamid Lahori's text was said to be unknown
  "until 1971"; now "until the 1960s".
- Chapter 4, p. 58, exercise 7: "Timurid and not their Mughal descent" corrected to "Mongol
  descent".
- Chapter 4, p. 57: the "Kings and queens" box on Akbar's contemporaries dropped Elizabeth I
  of England (eight sentences) and gained a box on Sultan Suleyman of Ottoman Turkey.
- Chapter 5, p. 62: temple captions were re-set; the 2007 PDF's captions are fragmentary in
  the text layer, so the deletions reported there are extraction artefacts, not edits.

### 2013 reprint to 2019 edition (the December 2017 review)

This is the edition that NCERT's 2017-18 textbook review produced. The 2018 captures are
word-for-word this text.

- Chapter 2, p. 21: "He was interested in finding out more about the people he conquered"
  became "Sultan Mahmud was also interested in finding out..."; "Kitab-al Hind" respelt
  "Kitab ul-Hind"; "al-Biruni" capitalised.
- Chapter 3, p. 36: the Qutb Minar caption corrected from "built by three Sultans: Qutbuddin
  Aybak, Iltutmish and Firuz Shah Tughluq" to "two Sultans: Qutbuddin Aybak and Iltutmish".
  "Suri dynasty" became "Sur dynasty" (p. 42).
- Chapter 4, p. 47: a new caption on gunpowder and firearms ("brought to India for warfare in
  the 14th century ... used by Babur"). p. 55: a new box on Akbar's translation bureau
  (Maktab Khana) and the Razmnamah.
- Chapter 5, p. 61: a new caption on Raniji ki Baori, Bundi. p. 65: section heading "Why were
  Temples Destroyed?" became "Why were Temples Targeted?", and "he also attacked the temples
  of defeated kings" lost "also".
- Chapter 6, p. 83: "Moors (a name used collectively for Muslim merchants)" became "Muslim
  merchants"; new captions on Vijayanagara tanks and canals. p. 88: the Vasco da Gama note
  was rewritten (route via the Cape of Good Hope).
- Chapter 8: "Guru Nanak" became "Baba Guru Nanak" in every occurrence (eleven sentences).
  New boxes on the Varkari sect and Vitthala (p. 108), the Rishi order of Sufism in Kashmir
  (p. 111), Shankaradeva and the Eka Sarana Nama Dharma (p. 114), and the bhakti saints'
  contribution to music (p. 115). p. 110: "It also rejected idol worship and considerably
  simplified rituals ... At the same time Muslim scholars developed a holy law called Shariat"
  was replaced by "In the eighth and ninth centuries religious scholars developed different
  aspects of the Holy Law (Shariat) and theology of Islam. While the religion of Islam
  gradually became more complex, Sufis provided it with an additional dimension...".
  "Janeshwar" respelt "Dnyaneshwar (Gyaneshwar)", "Sakkubai" to "Sakhubai".
- Chapter 10: new material on Rajput forts (Chittorgarh), Rana Pratap's defiance of the
  Mughals (p. 146), Sawai Jai Singh's observatories (p. 147), a biography box of Shivaji
  (p. 149), Baji Rao I (p. 150), and Suraj Mal of Bharatpur (p. 151). Exercise wording:
  "the Awadh nawabi" became "the Awadh state", "Deccan subadari" became "Hyderabad state".
  Captions of Mehrangarh Fort and Guru Gobind Singh were replaced.

### 2019 edition to rationalised 2023-24

Matches NCERT's own booklet (`docs/sources.md`) and adds the renames:

- Chapters 5 (Rulers and Buildings) and 6 (Towns, Traders and Craftspersons) dropped.
  Remaining chapters renumbered and cross-references rewritten ("Chapters 5, 6 and 7" to
  "Chapter 5", and so on).
- Chapter 3 retitled "Delhi: 12th to 15th Century"; chapter 4 "The Mughals (16th to 17th
  Century)"; chapter 1 gained "Introduction:".
- Chapter 2, p. 21: the paragraph on Mahmud of Ghazni lost "He ruled from 997 to 1030...",
  the Al-Biruni and Kitab ul-Hind sentences, and the glossary entry for "Sultan". The
  sentence "He raided the subcontinent almost every year – his targets were wealthy temples"
  became "His targets were wealthy temples" plus a new sentence: "He raided the subcontinent
  17 times (1000–1025) with a religious motive." "Rajendra I ... raided the Ganga valley"
  became "invaded". "Rich peasants of the Vellala caste" lost "of the Vellala caste". The
  "Elsewhere" box on Tang China was dropped (p. 28).
- Chapter 3: the whole section "From Garrison Town to Empire: The Expansion of the Delhi
  Sultanate" (pp. 34 to 35, 20 sentences) and the box "The Masjid" (p. 36, 7 sentences) were
  dropped, as were the comparison of Alauddin Khalji and Muhammad Tughluq's measures
  (pp. 40 to 42) and the "Elsewhere" box on the Three Orders, Knights and the Crusades
  (p. 43).
- Chapter 4: the table of Mughal emperors' campaigns (pp. 48 to 49) and every sentence
  referring to it, the box asking whether the painting suggests kingship as a birthright,
  the "A Closer Look: Akbar's Policies" section on provincial administration (p. 53), and the
  passage on Akbar's discussions at the ibadat khana leading to sulh-i kul (p. 54, 13
  sentences including the "Dogma" and "Bigot" glossary entries) were dropped. The Suleyman
  box (p. 57) went too.
- Chapter 7 (now 5): the "Elsewhere" box on the Mongols dropped (p. 101).
- Chapter 8 (now 6): the Martin Luther box dropped (p. 119).
- Chapter 9 (now 7): the box on the emergence of nation-states in Europe dropped (p. 135);
  "Sometimes, they figure as the 'cause' for conflicts, as men fought with one another to
  either 'win' or 'protect' women" dropped (p. 125).
- Chapter 10 (now 8): the sections on the old Mughal provinces, Hyderabad, Awadh and Bengal
  (pp. 142 to 146, about 60 sentences) and the "Elsewhere" box on the French Revolution
  (p. 152) dropped, along with the exercises that referred to them.
- Throughout: commas inserted after introductory phrases (about 50 sentences). Reported as
  `punctuation`, not as edits.

### Rationalised 2023-24 to 2024-25 printing

This pair is the least clean: the 2024-25 files set transliterated terms as outlines, which
are recovered by OCR, and use a two-column layout on some pages. Hunks containing stray
symbols are reading errors. Edits that are clearly real:

- Chapter 2, p. 21: Muhammad Ghori is "a Turk ruler" instead of "an Afghan ruler".
- Chapter 3: a new box on Khums, Zakat and Jizyah under the Sultanate, citing "Administrative
  system of Delhi Sultanat, Kitab Mahal Publishers" (pp. 35 to 36, about 10 sentences). The
  Raziyya box was expanded with Rudramadevi of Warangal and Didda of Kashmir and the
  heading "What Minhaj-us-Siraj thought about Raziyya: What other important female rulers of
  India did?" (p. 33). A quotation from Fakhr-i Mudabbir on kings, soldiers and revenue was
  added (p. 32). "Minhaj-i Siraj" became "Minhaj-us-Siraj".
- Chapter 4, p. 57: a new box "Lachit Barphukan and the defeat of the Mughals in Assam".
- Chapter 7, p. 84: a new glossary note on the term "classical".
- Chapter 8, pp. 101 to 102: "The Maratha kingdom was established by Chhatrapati Shivaji
  Maharaj (1630)" and a table of Maratha rulers from Shivaji to Maharani Tarabai and Shahu.
- Chapter 1, p. 8: "Other groups of people such as the Marathas, Sikhs, Jats, Ahoms and
  Kayasthas..." is reported as modified, but the 2024 sentence is interleaved with a
  neighbouring column in the extraction. Check the page image before citing it.

The 2024-25 printing is byte-identical to the live file on ncert.nic.in in October 2026.

### 2019 edition to the 2022-23 printing (OCR)

A sentence-level fuzzy comparison found no reworded sentence that survives checking. The
differences the engine reports are reading errors, split sentences, and exercise layout.
Treat this printing as the 2019 text.

## Our Pasts III (Class 8)

### 2008 text (2014 and 2016 captures) to 2018 reprint (2019 capture)

The 2018 files have headings and boxes in fonts without a Unicode map, so most reported
deletions are `unreadable`, not edits. Not analysed further until that edition is OCR'd.

### 2018 reprint to 2019 edition (2020 capture)

- Chapters 6 (Colonialism and the City: The Story of an Imperial Capital) and 10 (The
  Changing World of Visual Arts) dropped, leaving 10 chapters. This is the 2019 revision,
  separate from the 2023 rationalisation.
- The remaining hunks are dominated by text that is unreadable in the 2018 files.

### 2019 edition through 2021 and 2022 printings

Three edits in two years:

- Chapter 5, p. 56: Mangal Pandey "was hanged to death" on "29 March 1857" corrected to
  "8 April 1857" (2021).
- Chapter 3, p. 27: "Before 1865, the Company had purchased goods in India by importing gold
  and silver" corrected to "Before 1765" (2022).
- Chapter 4, p. 43: "Nishi tribes" respelt "Nyishi" (2022).

### 2022 printing to rationalised 2023-24

- Chapters 6 (Weavers, Iron Smelters and Factory Owners) and 10 (India After Independence)
  dropped, leaving 8.
- Every "Elsewhere" box was dropped: the slave trade in South Africa (ch. 2, p. 24), indigo
  making in the West Indies (ch. 3, p. 37), "Why do we need cash!" (ch. 4, p. 49), the Taiping
  Rebellion (ch. 5, p. 63), education as a civilising mission in England (ch. 7, p. 92), black
  slaves and white planters in America, with Lincoln's Gettysburg Address (ch. 8, p. 107),
  and nationalism in Ghana (ch. 9, p. 126).
- Chapter 5: the sentences on Khurda and the Paika rebellion changed "Odisha" to "Orissa"
  (six places), the opposite direction from most spelling updates.
- Chapter 2, p. 14: the Nawab's 1733 complaint was shortened ("Rs 100,000 ... carry great
  number of the king's subjects of both sexes into slavery" survives only in part).
- Chapter 8, p. 108: "How did Jyotirao, and other reformer justify their criticism of caste
  inequality" (grammar regressed).
- Commas inserted throughout (117 sentences, reported as `punctuation`).

### Rationalised 2023-24 to 2024-25 printing

- Chapter 5 retitled "When People Revolt: 1857 and After"; "rebellion" became "uprising" in
  fourteen sentences and the heading "A Mutiny Becomes a Popular Rebellion" became "...Popular
  Uprising".
- Chapter 4, pp. 48 to 49: "Hindu landlords" became "landlords" in all three places where
  the Birsa Munda movement's targets are listed. A caption for a Birsa Munda postage stamp
  was added.
- Chapter 2: "Seringapatam" became "Shrirangapatnam" (three places), "Benaras" became
  "Banaras", "nabobs" gained "(nawabs)", and "the Third Anglo-Maratha War of 1817–19 crushed
  Maratha power" became "the Maratha power came to end with the Third Anglo-Maratha War".

## Social and Political Life III (Class 8 civics)

### 2008 text (2014 capture) to December 2014 printing (2016 capture)

The 2014 capture is the 2008 first-edition text (A4-sized files). The next printing updated
data and exercises throughout:

- Chapter 1: "three organs of the State" became "three organs of government" (three places);
  "Om Prakash Valmiki" respelt "Omprakash"; "changed over the years" became "amended"; a new
  activity on the 11 Fundamental Duties; the glossary entry "Trafficking" became "Human
  Trafficking".
- Chapter 3: the table of Lok Sabha results was replaced (1984 results out, 2014 results in);
  "an increase in political participation from the Dalit and backward castes and the
  minorities" became "from the Dalits and backward classes"; "plus 2 nominated" became
  "plus 2 Anglo-Indian nominated".
- Chapter 5: the Chief Justice's speech on judicial vacancies is now dated 26 November 2014
  with a new vacancy table; new activities on vacancies, on Fundamental Rights, and on the
  Right to Food poster.
- Chapter 6: the opening on the police and fair trials was rewritten around Article 22 ("every
  person has a Fundamental Right to be defended by a lawyer"); the Shanti Hembram storyboard
  and the Fiesta Club activity were expanded.
- Chapter 7: the Muslim marginalisation data became a set of boxes with 2001 census figures
  and a comparison table of Hindus, Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists and Jains in
  government jobs; "Adivasi religions themselves have influenced dominant religions" kept.
- Chapter 8: a glossary (Confront, Ostracise) and activities on the Safai Karamchari
  Andolan's PIL were added; the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act got its own heading.

### December 2014 printing to December 2018 printing (2019 capture)

- Chapter 1: Nepal's constitution-writing is now past tense ("the people of Nepal adopted a
  new Constitution for the country in 2015"); the quoted article renumbered 37 to 75.
- Chapter 3: women MPs "just above nine per cent" became "just above eleven per cent"; a 2016
  National Plan of Action for Children answer was added as the sample parliamentary question.
- Chapter 5: the vacancy table updated to November 2018 and the 2014 speech removed;
  exercises renumbered.

### 2018 printing through 2020, 2021 and 2022 printings

- 2020: the Lok Sabha results table updated from the 16th (2014) to the 17th (2019) election,
  with the turnout row "17th 2019 67.11"; the vacancy table to 2019; "Domestic violence
  generally refers to".
- 2021: the Opposition "is formed by all the political parties that oppose the majority
  party/coalition" became "that are not part of the majority party/coalition"; the glossary
  example "If the government decides not to promote an officer of the armed forces for being
  a woman" was cut from the definition of tyranny; national park figures updated.
- 2022: only exercise numbering and a schemes table re-set. These are the 2022-23 session
  files (every chapter carries that footer); the February 2023 capture re-served them.

### November 2021 printing to rationalised 2023-24

- Chapters 4 (Understanding Laws) and 6 (Understanding Our Criminal Justice System)
  dropped; chapter 3 became "Parliament and the Making of Laws", absorbing the domestic
  violence law story and the "Unpopular and Controversial Laws" section from old chapter 4.
- Chapter 3, p. 39 to 40: the section "Who are the People in Parliament?" was dropped (about
  20 sentences): that Parliament "now has more and more people from different backgrounds",
  the reservation of seats for SCs and STs, the debate on reserving seats for women ("Sixty
  years ago, only four per cent of MPs were women and today it is just above eleven per
  cent"), and "Why do you think there are so few women in Parliament? Discuss."
- Chapter 7 (now 5), p. 90: the first-person account of being told to change out of a
  sherwani and salwar kameez during Ramzan was dropped, as were the references to the Ansari
  family's difficulty renting a house and to Kanta's story from the Class VII book.
- Chapter 5 (now 4), p. 55: the Hakim Sheikh case (Right to Life includes Right to Health)
  dropped. Chapter 1, p. 7: the Omprakash Valmiki references dropped.

### Rationalised 2023-24 to 2024-25 printing

- Chapter 5, p. 65: "Adivasis practise a range of tribal religions that are different from
  Islam, Hinduism and Christianity" became "a range of belief systems that are different from
  other religions".
- Chapter 5, p. 71 to 72: the Sachar Committee passage was recast. "It suggests that on a
  range of ... indicators the situation of the Muslim community is comparable to that of
  other marginalised communities" became "The report discussed in detail ... and suggested";
  "treat them unfairly" became "treat them differently"; "this prejudice leads to hatred and
  violence" became "has also led to"; "there is a link between economic and social
  marginalisation" became "there used to be a link". National park figures updated to the
  Wildlife Institute of India's November 2023 data; "marginalised" respelt "marginalized" in
  that passage.
- Chapter 3, p. 36: "(plus 2 Anglo-Indian nominated)" removed from the Lok Sabha count,
  following the 104th Amendment.
- Chapter 4: vacancy table updated again; Teacher's Note rewritten for the merged unit.
