# Review of social-political-life-2 (Social and Political Life II, Class 7)

Method: for every item both editions' pages were rendered from the PDFs and looked at. Verdicts follow review/README.md. Short names used for patterns:

- Rule A: blue side-note boxes and mock-page/comic "[table]" blocks are split, merged or ordered differently between editions even though the page shows identical text.
- Rule B: a figure caption or a page-bound side element is spliced into a running sentence (or the sentence is cut at the page break) in one edition only.
- Rule C: source PDF set differs between editions (pages missing from every chapter file of one edition, or a separate references file never ingested).

Rules referred to in the entries:

- A: comic speech bubbles, mock newspaper columns, table cells, blue side-note boxes and "[table]" blocks are cut and ordered differently per edition.
- B: a photo caption or box is spliced into a sentence that runs across a page or column break.
- C: source gaps (2016 chapter files lack printed pages 16-17, the 2014 references sit in a separate PDF, the map page after the References, chapter-9 content filed under chapter 8).
- D: digits and labels inside maps, charts and illustrations are read as body, note or heading text.
- E: exercise lines, blue margin statements and glossary lines leak into each other's streams.
- H: the rupee sign is read as a backtick (2019 to 2020 PDFs) or as one of "¥ = z ?" (2022 OCR).
- I: OCR of white text on colour bands, text inside pictures, QR labels and vertical "CHAPTER" labels.
- J: the second line (URL) of an SDG picture caption leaves its caption.
- K to P: dash and accent normalisation in OCR, missing exercise numbers, glued words, "|" for "I", unit-opener pages filed under different chapters.

Three patterns that would remove the most noise if fixed by a rule:

1. Rule A, boxes, tables and comics as units. Extract each blue side-note box, each table cell, each speech bubble and each mock newspaper column as one block in reading order (by bounding box), and compare these blocks as bags of sentences. This alone accounts for roughly a third of the false hunks in every pair (the comic pages 22-23 of chapter 2, 31 of chapter 3, 47 of chapter 4, 58 of chapter 5, the newspaper page 74 / 68 of chapter 6, and every blue box in chapters 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8).
2. Rules B and E, stream and page-break joins. Join a sentence that ends at the foot of a page or column before inserting any caption of the next page (Rule B, seen in chapter 1 pages 7, 11-12, chapter 2 pages 24-25 and 25-26, chapter 4 page 45-46, chapter 6 page 74, chapter 8 pages 99-100 and 93-94), and keep exercises, their blue margin statements and the glossary in separate streams by x position (Rule E, seen in every chapter's exercises page). These two produce many of the "modified" and "deleted/inserted" pairs that reappear in the same place in a different edition each time.
3. Rules H, I and D, symbols and picture text. Map the rupee sign (backtick, "¥", "=", "z", "?" before a number) to a single "₹" in chapters 6 to 9 and normalise "Rs" if wanted (this removes about 40 hunks in 2019 and the entire currency noise in 2022), drop OCR lines that come from inside pictures and posters (QR labels "0764CH09", vertical "CHAPTER", map labels, chart axis digits, cotton-boll digits, the poster on chapter 8 page 98 / chapter 9 page 112) and re-read white text on colour bands (UNIT and EXERCISES and Glossary headings) so the 2022 OCR matches the other editions.
